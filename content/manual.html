Chapter 1Before you start

Hearken Studio listens to the sermon through the church’s sound and puts each scripture on the screen as it’s said or read. It runs on the computer at the media desk and needs no internet during the service.

What you need

  • A computer: a Mac (macOS 11 or later, Apple Silicon or Intel), a Windows 10 or 11 PC, or a 64-bit Linux PC. The laptop you already use for slides is usually enough.
  • The sound of the service: a cable from the sound desk is best. A microphone in the room also works.
  • A screen for the congregation: a projector or TV connected as a second display.
  • The internet, once: to sign in and to download anything extra (Bibles in other languages, larger speech models). After that, Hearken works offline for up to 30 days at a time.

Install Hearken

  1. Download the installer for your computer from the download section.
  2. Mac: open the .dmg and drag Hearken Studio into Applications. The first time you open it, macOS may say it can’t check the developer. Open System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  3. Windows: run the .exe installer. If SmartScreen appears, click More info → Run anyway. IT admins can use the .msi instead.
  4. Linux: use the .deb (Ubuntu, Debian), the .rpm (Fedora, openSUSE) or the AppImage, which runs on any distribution once you allow it to run as a program.

The first time you open it

Two short screens get you ready. You can change everything on them later in Settings.

  1. Choose the languages preached at your church. English is ticked already. Tick Français or Español as well if your services use them; tick more than one if they mix languages.
  2. If you ticked French or Spanish, choose whether Hearken’s menus and buttons change to that language too, or stay in English.
  3. Click Continue. Anything the languages need (their Bible and a speech model) downloads in the background.
  4. Sign in, or click New to Hearken? Create an account and enter the church name, an email and a password (at least 8 characters). You can also click Not now and sign in when you first start listening.

Your account, free trial and licence

Every computer gets a free trial of 60 days with every feature. The chip next to the logo shows the days left. Click it, or open Settings → Account and licence, to see the details.

  • Signing in needs the internet. After that Hearken keeps working offline for up to 30 days, and renews the sign-in by itself whenever it’s online.
  • Check now renews the sign-in and checks the computer’s date with the Hearken website. Nothing about your services is sent.
  • Licence key: paste the key from your email (it starts with HK1.) and click Activate.
  • Only starting to listen needs an account and a trial or licence. Search, the screen and phones keep working without one, and a service that is already listening is never stopped.

Chapter 2A tour of the screen

Everything you need during the service is on one screen.

  • Top bar: start and stop listening, the sound input, the translation, and the Share, Screens, Settings and Open output buttons.
  • Detections (left): every scripture or song Hearken hears, newest first, with how sure it is.
  • Preview and Live (centre): Preview is what you’re about to show; Live is what the congregation sees. Go live sits between them.
  • Search and the passage picker (under the monitors): find any passage yourself.
  • Transcript (bottom left): what’s being said, line by line.
  • Plan, Songs, Archive (bottom right): the sermon plan, your song library, and past services.

The top bar, left to right

  1. Trial or sign-in chip: days left on the trial, or Sign in. Click it for your account.
  2. Start listening: shows Listening and a timer while it runs. Click it again to stop.
  3. Sound input and its level meter.
  4. Translation (KJV, WEB…) and + Side by side for a second translation.
  5. Share: phones, livestream overlay and the phone remote. A gold dot means sharing is on.
  6. Screens: extra screens such as a stage display. A gold dot means one is open.
  7. Settings: style, accuracy, Bibles, language, account and the options used during the service.
  8. Open output: opens the projector window.

Chapter 3Getting ready for Sunday

Connect the sound

The clearer the sound, the faster and more accurate Hearken is. A direct feed from the desk beats a microphone in the room.

  1. Run a cable from the desk’s aux, record or matrix output into the computer’s audio input, or into a USB audio interface.
  2. Choose it in the sound input menu in the top bar. The list refreshes when you point at it, so devices plugged in later appear.
  3. Ask someone to speak at the pulpit and watch the level meter beside the menu. The dot lights when speech is heard; the bar turns red when the sound is too loud.

Tip: If the meter barely moves, raise the send on the desk rather than the computer’s input volume. The input can’t be changed while listening: stop listening first.

To listen to the computer’s own audio instead, for example a livestream feed: on Windows choose an input marked 🔁; on a Mac install a virtual audio device such as BlackHole and choose it.

Connect the projector

  1. Connect the projector or TV as a second display (extended, not mirrored).
  2. Click Open output. The verse window opens full screen on the second display.
  3. To use a different display, open Settings and choose it under Output display. Choose it again after restarting Hearken.
  4. To close the projector window, click on it and press Esc, or use Settings → Close the output window. Open it again at any time: it shows what’s live straight away.

No second display? On a laptop on its own, the output opens as an ordinary window you can move and resize, which is handy for practising.

Choose the translation

  1. Pick the Bible in the Translation menu in the top bar. KJV, WEB, ASV, BBE and YLT are included; Bibles you download appear here too.
  2. To show two at once, pick a second one in + Side by side. Choose Side by side again at the top of that menu to go back to one.
  3. With two translations, a small button appears next to them: click it to stack them one above the other instead of side by side.

Paste the sermon plan

If the pastor shares notes or a list of passages, give them to Hearken before the service. Every planned passage is ready to show in one click, and Hearken is more confident when it hears one.

  1. Open the Plan tab at the bottom right.
  2. Paste the notes or verse list as they are, for example “Intro: John 3:16, 1. Rom 8:28-30, 2. Psalm 23”.
  3. Click Line up references. Each passage becomes a row below, and the count shows how many were found.
  4. During the service, click a row to preview it, or click Live (or double-click the row) to show it.

Clear plan empties it for next week.

Teach it your names

Names Hearken has never heard (your pastors, the church, local places) can come out wrong. Add them once:

  1. Open Settings → Transcription accuracy.
  2. Under Names and words to listen for, type each name or phrase, separated by commas or one per line. Bible book names and today’s plan are added automatically.
  3. Click outside the box to save. It takes effect straight away.

Before-service checklist

  • Sound input chosen, and the meter moves when someone speaks.
  • Projector connected, Open output clicked, the right display showing the verse.
  • Translation chosen.
  • Sermon plan pasted, if there is one.
  • Sharing turned on if members follow on phones or you use the remote or a livestream.
  • Start listening clicked a few minutes before the sermon.

Chapter 4During the service

Start listening

  1. Click Start listening. The button changes to Listening with a timer.
  2. The very first time, Hearken spends a few seconds testing your computer to choose the fastest settings. This happens once.
  3. Click Listening again to stop when the service ends.

The Hearken logo in the top bar gently pulses while it listens.

Detection cards

Each scripture or song Hearken hears becomes a card in Detections, newest at the top.

A card shows the passage, how sure Hearken is (the bar and percentage), the words it heard, and how it was found:

  • Announced: the reference was said, such as “John three sixteen”.
  • Follow-up: “verse 18” or “chapter 4”, understood from the passage before.
  • Quoted or Possible quote: the verse was read or quoted without a reference.
  • Same meaning: the verse was said in other words.
  • Song: a song from your library is being sung.
  • Voice command: the preacher said “next verse” or similar (see voice commands).

Small labels add more: in plan (it’s in the sermon plan), shown automatically, and ON SCREEN in red for what’s live.

  1. Click a card to put it in Preview.
  2. Double-click it, or click Live, to put it straight on screen.
  3. Click Dismiss to remove a card you don’t need.

Preview follows latest (top of the list) puts each new detection into Preview as it arrives. It switches itself off when you click a card, so your choice isn’t replaced.

Preview and live

  • Go live (or Enter) puts what’s in Preview on screen.
  • ◀ Verse and Verse ▶ move the live passage one verse back or on, within the chapter. On a song they move between parts.
  • Black blacks out the screen; click Unblack to bring it back.
  • Clear takes the verse off the screen.

Showing sure detections automatically

Hearken can put the references it’s very sure of on screen by itself, so the screen keeps up even when the desk is busy. It’s off until you turn it on.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tick Show sure detections automatically, at and set how sure it must be: 0.9 (90%) is a good start. Higher is more careful.
  • Verses read without a reference go up on their own only when Hearken is at least 92% sure.
  • Verses said in other words (Same meaning) are only ever suggested, never shown by themselves.
  • A song goes up by itself only onto a clear screen or in place of another song, and a scripture never replaces a live song by itself.

Follow-along reading

When a passage is live and someone reads it aloud, the words light up on the screen as they’re read, and the next verse goes up as the reader reaches it, on into the next chapter. Turn it on or off in Settings → Follow reading; change the highlight colour (or turn it off) in Style → Highlight words read.

Voice commands

The preacher can move the live scripture by saying so. Turn them on or off in Settings → Voice commands. They only act on a finished sentence, only while a scripture (not a song) is live, and never while the room is singing.

“Next verse.”also: “Next.”, “Go to the next verse.”, “Go forward.” “Go back.”also: “Previous verse.”, “Go to the previous verse.” “Go to verse 6.”also: “Read verse 6.”, “Go to the tenth verse.” “Let’s read verses 6 to 8.”shows that range “Go to the first verse.”back to the start of the passage “Let’s go to the final verse.”the last verse of the chapter

Hearken ignores the same words when they’re part of the sermon, such as “in the next verse Paul says…” or “let’s go back to Genesis 3”. In French and Spanish, phrases like “Verset suivant”, “Allons au verset 6”, “Versículo siguiente” and “Vamos al versículo 6” work too.

The transcript

  • Each line is what was said, with the time. The line being spoken has a cursor.
  • ✓ means a larger model has re-checked the line (see accuracy).
  • → John 3:16 shows what the line led to; ↪ Next verse marks a voice command.
  • ♪ Singing appears at the top while the room sings. Sung lines are never taken as quotes or commands, and are left out of sermon notes.

Chapter 5Finding a passage yourself

Search

Type in the search box under Preview. It understands references and words alike:

  • A reference, written any way: “jn 3 16”, “1 cor 13”, “psalm 23”.
  • Words from the verse: “love is patient”.
  • An idea in your own words: “don’t worry about tomorrow”. Verses that say the same thing appear under By meaning once that feature is downloaded (see accuracy; English only).
  1. Click a result, or press Enter for the first one, to put it in Preview.
  2. Press Enter again to put it on screen.
  3. Esc clears the box.

The passage picker

Next to search are boxes for Book, Ch. (chapter), V. (verse) and to (last verse).

  1. Start typing the book and press Enter or Tab to accept it.
  2. Type the chapter and press Enter, then the verse. Add a last verse for a range, or leave the verse empty for the whole chapter.
  3. Preview updates as you type. Press Enter to show it, or ⌘ Enter (Mac) / Ctrl Enter (Windows, Linux) to show it straight away.

A slide holds up to 6 verses. For a longer passage, use Verse ▶ to move through the rest.

Chapter 6Worship songs

Hearken can recognise the songs in your library as they’re sung and follow them part by part. It comes with no lyrics: add the songs your church is licensed to project.

Add a song

  1. Open the Songs tab at the bottom right and click + Add.
  2. Paste the lyrics, for example from CCLI SongSelect. The first line is the title.
  3. Separate parts with a blank line, and start each part with its name: “Verse 1”, “Chorus”, “Bridge”, “Pre-chorus”, “Tag”, “Outro” and so on. Parts without a name become “Part 1”, “Part 2”.
  4. Click Save song.

A line such as “CCLI Song # 1234567” is kept as the song’s CCLI number, and an “Author:” or “Words and music by” line as its author. Copyright lines at the end are removed. Long parts are split across slides of up to 6 lines.

Import song files

Click Import in the Songs tab and choose one or more .txt files laid out the same way.

Show and follow a song

  • Automatically: with Settings → Follow worship songs on, a song being sung appears as a Song card. Show it, and the parts move on as they’re sung.
  • By hand: click a song in the list, then Live on the part you want. ← and → move between parts.

If the room sings for a while and the song isn’t in your library, Hearken tells you, so you can add it for next time. Click a song, then Edit or Delete, to change it.

CCLI song-use report

At the bottom of the Songs tab, click Export song usage for CCLI (last 90 days). Hearken saves a spreadsheet file (.csv) in Documents → Hearken Studio and opens it, ready for your CCLI report.

Chapter 7Making the screen look right

Open Settings → Style. The style panel opens on the left. Your changes show in Preview only until you click Apply to screen above Preview, so you can experiment during a service. Discard throws them away.

  • Start from: ready-made looks: Classic Dark, Sunrise, Night sky, Clean light, Bold, Calvary.
  • Background: a plain colour, one of the sample pictures, or your own (click + Upload: JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF). With a picture, Darken and Soften keep the words readable.
  • Verse text: any font included with Hearken or installed on the computer, its weight, colour and largest size (text shrinks to fit), line spacing, alignment, shadow and outline. Tick Verse numbers to show them, and Highlight words read for the follow-along colour.
  • Reference (e.g. JOHN 3:16): show it above or below, its font, colour and size, in capitals or not, with or without the translation.
  • Layout: margins, two translations side by side or stacked, and how slides change (Fade or Cut, and how long the fade takes).

Advanced: edit as JSON shows the whole style as text you can copy to another computer or save as a file. Paste one in and click Use this JSON to use it.

Chapter 8More screens

Besides the main projector, Hearken can drive up to three more screens, each following what’s live. Click Screens in the top bar.

  1. Click + Add a screen.
  2. Choose what it Shows:
    • Stage display: for the preacher or worship leader: large text, what’s been read, whether the screen is live, black or clear, and the time.
    • Lower third: for a livestream or video switcher: the passage in a band at the bottom, over Black, Green or your theme colour (Behind it) so it can be keyed out.
    • Same as main: another projector or TV with the main look.
  3. Choose the Display, or leave it on Auto, and click Open.

Press Esc on a screen to close it, or use Close in the panel. One display can show only one screen; on a laptop alone, screens open as windows. For OBS or vMix on another computer, the livestream overlay is easier.

Chapter 9Phones, remote and livestream

Hearken can share the live verse over the church Wi-Fi: to members’ phones, to a phone remote for the operator, and to livestream software. No internet is needed, only the same Wi-Fi.

Turn on sharing

  1. Click Share in the top bar and tick Share on the local network.
  2. Choose a Network name, such as your church’s name. Phones will open http://yourname.local. A ✓ confirms phones can use it.
  3. If the computer asks whether to allow Hearken Studio through the firewall, allow it.

Some Android phones can’t open “.local” addresses. They can use the number address shown in the panel (such as http://192.168.1.24), and the QR code always works.

Members follow on their phones

  1. In the Share panel, click Show QR on screen. A box on the projector invites members to follow along on their phone.
  2. Members scan the code with their phone camera. The live verse opens in the browser: no app to install.
  3. Each person can pick their own translation (any Bible on the computer, including downloaded ones) and make the text bigger or smaller with A− and A+.
  4. Click Hide QR on screen when you’re done.

Recap on phones (in the Share panel) lets members read or download the service recap as PDF or Word: choose scriptures, songs and sermon notes, scriptures and songs only, or off.

The phone remote

Run the screen from anywhere in the room, for example from the front row.

  1. On your phone, open the Remote address shown in the Share panel (it ends in /remote).
  2. Enter the 4-digit PIN shown under it and tap Connect. The PIN changes each time Hearken starts.
  3. Use ◀ Previous, Next ▶, Blackout and Clear, or tap Show on anything detected or planned.

Livestream overlay (OBS, vMix)

  1. In the Share panel, click Copy next to Livestream overlay.
  2. In OBS or vMix, add a Browser source with that address, sized 1920 × 1080.
  3. The passage appears in a band at the bottom whenever something is live, and disappears when the screen is black or clear.

To place it at the top, add ?pos=top to the end of the address. The livestream computer must be on the same network.

Chapter 10After the service

Everything Hearken heard and showed is kept on the computer, in the Archive tab.

Search past sermons

Type a passage or a phrase, such as “Romans 8” or “mustard seed”, into the Archive search. Each result shows the date and whether it was said, shown or mentioned. Click one with a reference to preview it again.

Sermon recap

  1. In Recap heading, type your church’s name. It becomes the title of every recap.
  2. Next to the service, click Recap and choose PDF, Word (.docx), Word 97–2003 (.doc) or Web page.
  3. Hearken saves it in Documents → Hearken Studio and opens it.

A recap has the sermon notes (key points and themes), every scripture shown, others mentioned, and the songs. The notes are made on the computer itself; nothing is sent to an online service.

Insights

Under the services, Insights shows the year at a glance: how many services, scriptures shown, the balance of Old and New Testament, the books and passages preached most, and the most-sung songs. Use the arrows to change the year.

Chapter 11Bibles and languages

Download more Bibles

Open Settings → Bibles and click Download next to any Bible: Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Twi (Akuapem and Asante), Ewe, Swahili, French, Spanish, Portuguese or German. Each is about 4–5 MB and works offline once downloaded.

A downloaded Bible can be the main translation, the second one side by side, or the one a member picks on their phone. Book names appear in that language, with the publisher’s copyright notice where it’s required. Remove deletes one you no longer need.

Preaching in French or Spanish

Hearken listens to sermons in English, French and Spanish, including services that switch between them.

  1. Open Settings → Language.
  2. Tick every language preached at your church.
  3. Click Save. Hearken downloads that language’s Bible and a speech model that understands it. Downloads carry on if you close the window.

References are understood the way they’re said in each language, such as “Jean chapitre trois, verset seize” or “Primera de Corintios trece”. Changes apply the next time you start listening. Search by meaning works in English only for now.

Menus in French or Spanish

In the same window, choose whether Hearken’s menus and buttons stay in English or change to French or Spanish. The phone pages, stage display and recap headings stay in English for now.

Chapter 12Accuracy and speed

Hearken picks the best settings for your computer the first time you listen. You can fine-tune them in Settings → Transcription accuracy.

  • Accuracy pass: a fast model keeps the transcript quick, then a larger model re-checks each finished sentence (lines marked ✓) for better words, names and accents. Automatic is right for most computers; Off uses the quick transcription only.
  • Models: download a larger model for better accuracy. Small suits any recent computer; Large v3 Turbo is the most accurate and best with accents, and needs Apple Silicon or a graphics card. Test speed shows how it runs on your computer.
  • Verses said in other words: a 31 MB download that finds a verse when someone paraphrases it, live as a suggestion and under “By meaning” in search. Untick Suggest live to keep it to search only.
  • Names and words to listen for: see Teach it your names.

Model changes apply the next time you start listening. If you change computers or something feels slow, Settings → Re-benchmark (under Speech engine) tests the computer again; it’s available when you’re not listening.

Chapter 13Updates

When a new version is out, a banner at the top says so. Click Install & restart when it suits you, or Later. Updates never install by themselves, and can’t be installed while listening, so a service is never interrupted. See what changed in the changelog.

Chapter 14When something isn’t right

The meter doesn’t move, or “No sound is reaching Hearken Studio”

  • Check the right input is chosen, and that the desk is sending to it.
  • Mac: open System Settings → Privacy & Security → Microphone and allow Hearken Studio, then stop and start listening.
  • Windows: check Settings → Privacy → Microphone allows desktop apps.

Start listening asks me to sign in, or won’t start

  • Sign in: listening needs the church’s account. Connect to the internet and sign in.
  • Sign-in needs renewing: it’s been more than 30 days offline. Connect to the internet; it renews by itself, or click Check now.
  • Trial ended or Licence ended: enter a licence key under Settings → Account and licence.
  • “This computer’s date looks wrong”: set the correct date and time, or connect to the internet and click Check now.
  • A speech model is needed: open Settings → Language and click Download what’s missing.

A verse is missed or wrong

  • Improve the sound first: a direct desk feed makes the biggest difference.
  • Paste the sermon plan and add names that come out wrong.
  • Download a larger model under Transcription accuracy.
  • Keep automatic showing off, or raise its number, so you approve each card.

Phones can’t connect

  • Phones and the computer must be on the same Wi-Fi. Guest networks often keep devices apart.
  • Allow Hearken Studio in the computer’s firewall.
  • Use the number address or the QR code instead of the “.local” name.
  • If the address ends in :7878, include that part: Hearken uses it when the usual port is taken.

The verse opens on the wrong display

Choose the display under Settings → Output display and click Open output again. Make sure the projector is set to extend the desktop, not mirror it.

“Too slow on this computer for the accuracy pass”

Hearken has switched to quick transcription for this service. Open Settings → Transcription accuracy and choose Automatic or a smaller model.

Still stuck? Visit Support.

Chapter 15Your data and privacy

  • Sermons stay in the building. Speech is turned into text on the computer itself. Recordings, transcripts and notes are never uploaded.
  • What goes online: signing in, registering the trial, checking the date, downloading Bibles and models, and checking for updates. None of it includes what was said in your services.
  • Your files: recaps and CCLI reports are saved in Documents → Hearken Studio. Songs, settings, the archive and downloads stay in Hearken’s own folder on the computer.
  • Phones and the remote connect over your local Wi-Fi only, and the remote needs the PIN shown on the media desk.
